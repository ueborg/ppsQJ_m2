## Candidate C1 — the 0.96 was a genuine lambda_c amplitude, not an r_c mislabel

1. **Statement.** A = 0.96 was produced by an inverse-variance-weighted log-log
   fit of `lambda_c = A*zeta^phi` to six 1/sqrt(L)-extrapolated lambda_c values
   over zeta ≤ 0.3, in the lambda_c parameterization. It is not, and never was,
   an r_c-type prefactor.
2. **Strongest affirmative case.** The primary document
   `analysis/lambda_c_phi_analysis.md` (2026-05-19, tracked at `6c9c843`) fits
   lambda_c^inf explicitly and never forms r_c. Re-running its recipe on its own
   published table reproduces (phi, A) = (0.503, 0.965) against its quoted
   (0.502 ± 0.026, 0.96) — four-digit agreement. Independently reproduced by two
   investigators.
3. **Closest known precedent.** `CB-PARAM-001`, which establishes that
   lambda_c/r_c exponent gaps are pure Jacobian. That claim is what made the
   mislabel story *sound* plausible.
4. **Novelty objection.** None; this is provenance reconstruction, not a new
   result.
5. **Correctness objection.** The reproduction runs on the document's own output
   table, not its raw inputs, which are gone. A shared upstream error would be
   invisible.
6. **Practicality objection.** None. T0, minutes of compute.
7. **Significance objection.** The verdict (0.96 is wrong) does not change. Only
   the recorded reason changes. Is that worth a task?
8. **Decisive test.** Does the primary document form r_c anywhere? It does not.
9. **Kill criteria.** Find r_c in the May document, or fail to reproduce
   (phi, A) from its table.
10. **What survives.** All of it. Three independent agents, two of them
    reproducing the arithmetic.
11. **Stronger version.** The recorded reason is not merely unsupported but
    *contradicted*, and the claim file's `evidence_note` ("original analysis not
    located on disk") is factually wrong — the document is tracked in git.

**Status: SURVIVES.** Objection 7 is answered by C2.

## Candidate C2 — the amplitude is an artifact of an extrapolation exponent that assumed the answer

1. **Statement.** A = 0.96 and phi ≈ 0.502 were co-generated by choosing the
   crossing-extrapolation exponent p = 1/2, a choice justified by *assuming*
   nu = 2. The same data give A = 0.53, 0.66, 0.79, 0.96 for p = 2, 1, 0.7, 0.5.
2. **Strongest affirmative case.** The A-versus-p table is in the primary
   document itself. Dropping the single high-leverage point at zeta = 0.30
   collapses the fit to phi = 0.259, A = 0.406 and drops chi²/dof from ~13 to
   ~1.5.
3. **Closest precedent.** `CB-WINDOW-001` (window drift) and charter §6 warning
   6, "a theorem whose assumptions largely encode the desired conclusion".
4. **Novelty objection.** None claimed.
5. **Correctness objection.** Non-monotone per-zeta intercepts mean *no*
   extrapolation exponent is defensible here, including the ones that give
   "better" answers. The finding is that the quantity is undetermined, not that
   p = 2 is right.
6. **Practicality objection.** None.
7. **Significance objection.** None available. This is a live generative
   mechanism: the same p = 1/2 choice feeds `CB-PHI-HALF-001`, the project
   headline in `DISP-PHI-001`.
8. **Decisive test.** Vary p and the high-leverage point; observe whether A and
   phi move together. They do.
9. **Kill criteria.** A stable A across p ∈ [0.5, 2], or a principled derivation
   of p = 1/2 that does not presuppose nu = 2.
10. **What survives.** All of it.
11. **Stronger version.** The 0.96 episode and the phi = 1/2 headline are **the
    same error observed twice**, not two independent findings.

**Status: SURVIVES. This is the finding of the task.**

## Candidate C3 — an exponent-free endpoint argument excludes 0.96

1. **Statement.** Under a single-power ansatz holding to zeta = 1,
   A = lambda_c(1) identically for every phi. Measured lambda_c(1) = 0.43–0.49
   on two locators, so A = 0.96 is excluded by a factor of two without reference
   to any exponent.
2. **Strongest affirmative case.** Algebraically trivial and therefore robust;
   the measurement comes from `EV-DATA-BOUNDARYCSV-001`, the only dataset
   carrying zeta = 1.0.
3. **Closest precedent.** None internal — this endpoint constraint had not been
   used.
4. **Novelty objection.** It is elementary algebra. [J] Its value is that nobody
   applied it for three months.
5. **Correctness objection.** **It depends entirely on the zeta convention.** If
   zeta = 1 is not the Born/unpostselected endpoint, the argument is vacuous.
   That convention is recorded in `SRC-LMR-2025` as taken from project notes and
   **not verified against the paper**.
6. **Practicality objection.** None.
7. **Significance objection.** It only re-derives a conclusion already reached.
8. **Decisive test.** Read the zeta convention out of LMR.
9. **Kill criteria.** An inverted convention, or evidence that no single power
   reaches zeta = 1 (which the flatness at zeta ≥ 0.5 already suggests).
10. **What survives.** The arithmetic. The premise does not.
11. **Stronger version.** Demote to a consistency check pending source
    inspection, and note that the observed flatness of lambda_c for zeta ≥ 0.5
    independently undercuts the single-power premise.

**Status: SURVIVES WITH SCOPE RESTRICTION** — contingent on an uninspected
source. Recorded as such rather than used.

11. **Stronger version.** Two of four plausible extrapolation variants land
    outside `CB-AMP-001`'s own falsifier band. **The superseding claim is now
