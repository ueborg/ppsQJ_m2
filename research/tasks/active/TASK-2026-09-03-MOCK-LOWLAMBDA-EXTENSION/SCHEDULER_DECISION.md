# Scheduler decision: cpu_med on all three arms

## The decision

All three arms request **`cpu_med`**, with `--array=0-95%64`.
`shared/preflight.py` **requires** it and exits non-zero on anything else
(`REQUIRED_PARTITION = "cpu_med"`; negative controls N10 and N11).

## This reverses the predecessor's rule, deliberately

`TASK-2026-09-02-MOCK-PRODUCTION` chose, per arm, the *smallest* partition whose
`MaxTime` covered the request:

> `PARTITION -- the SMALLEST valid partition for this arm's request, chosen from
> the request, not inherited from any predecessor.`

Under that rule two of this task's three arms would go to `cpu_short`:

| arm | `--time` | cpu_short MaxTime | smallest-fitting partition |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `lowlamL32` | `00:20:00` | 1 h | `cpu_short` |
| `lowlamL48` | `00:45:00` | 1 h | `cpu_short` |
| `lowlamL64` | `02:00:00` | 1 h | `cpu_med` |

They do not. The rule optimises the wrong quantity.

## Why

The preceding campaign demonstrated that on this account **`cpu_short` is
effectively serialised by `QOSMaxJobsPerUserLimit`**, while `cpu_med` delivered
real parallelism. A `%64` cap is a cap, not a grant: on `cpu_short` the QOS
limit binds first and the array drains close to one task at a time, so the
`--array` concurrency the cost model assumes never materialises.

The arithmetic is stark. `lowlamL48` is 96 tasks at ~9.6 min each:

| | elapsed |
|---|---:|
| `cpu_med`, 64 concurrent (two waves) | **19.2 min** |
| `cpu_short`, effectively serialised | ~15.4 h |

and `lowlamL64` is 96 tasks at ~28.3 min:

| | elapsed |
|---|---:|
| `cpu_med`, 64 concurrent (two waves) | **56.6 min** |
| `cpu_short`, effectively serialised | ~45 h |

A "smallest partition" rule would therefore turn a one-hour campaign into a
multi-day one while requesting *less* of the cluster, which is not a saving in
any sense that matters.

## What is given up

`cpu_med` is a coarser-grained resource, so a very short job may sit longer in
the queue before it starts than the same job would on `cpu_short`. That is a
real cost and it is accepted: the alternative is not a shorter queue wait but a
serialised run.

Nothing else changes. The `--time` limits are still sized from measured runtime
with substantial margin (`COST_MODEL.md` §6) rather than inflated to fill the
partition — `lowlamL32` asks for 20 minutes on a 4-hour partition. Requesting
`cpu_med` is a statement about *which queue*, not a licence to over-request
*time*.

## What to check on the day

`RUCHE_RUNBOOK.md` §2 gives the accounting check. If the allocation turns out to
grant 64 concurrent slots **in total across all three arrays** rather than per
array, lower `%64` to `%21` on each. The `%N` cap is the only number in these
scripts that is safe to hand-edit; everything else is regenerated by
`tools/build_arms.py`.

If `cpu_med` itself turns out to be QOS-limited for this account, that is new
information about the cluster and not a reason to fall back to `cpu_short` —
report it and stop, because the cost model's parallelism assumption would then
be wrong everywhere in the programme, not just here.
